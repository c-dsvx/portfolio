About

I am a third-year PhD candidate in cognitive science, based in the education research group at the University of Placeholder. My work sits at the intersection of metacognition, motivation and instructional design.

Broadly, I ask how students come to regulate their own learning: how they set goals, choose strategies, judge whether something is working, and change course when it is not. I combine classroom field studies with controlled experiments and fine-grained process data — think log files, think-aloud protocols, and self-report traces collected over time.

Beyond the lab, I care about translating this work into practice, and I write and speak regularly for teachers, students and the wider public. A short summary of my research, teaching and outreach follows; my full CV is available as a PDF.
